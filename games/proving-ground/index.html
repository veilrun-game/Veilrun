How to play

Waves come through the seam and do not stop. Wound them, then Execute — a finisher refunds a Veilstep, so kills feed the next kill. Every Execute thins the floor where it lands, and husks move faster over thin ground: the score and the danger come from the same place.

Camera

Zoom is a different quantity in each view — arcade pulls the camera in, third person shortens the boom, first person opens the lens. Scroll the wheel — or press − and = — and it moves whichever of these three the view you are in uses. 0 puts it back.
Faces where you move — he turns to face where you steer, and the camera orbits freely, right round to his face. Move and look apart keeps running you the way you were going while the camera swings anywhere: look behind you without turning round. It re-aims to the screen the moment you stop. Aim with the mouse points your strikes wherever the mouse is, while he still turns to run the way you steer — back up and he walks toward you rather than sliding away from you.
Faces where you move is arcade’s default — the camera is fixed, so walking is aiming. Aim with the mouse keeps the fixed camera and puts your strikes on the mouse, while he still turns to run the way you steer.
First person always faces where you look and moves sideways when you strafe. There is nothing to choose here, so there is no control.
A phone's window is nearly square, so the arena crops at the sides. 0 keeps the tuned telephoto and the biggest character; 1 widens the lens until you see as much arena as a desktop does.

Controls

Mirrors the whole pad, and mirrors the button columns with it, so Strike and Execute stay under the thumb that is not steering.
Scales the whole pad together — stick, buttons and the camera stick. Bigger targets cost arena: the gameplay window is whatever the pad leaves.
A small stick above the buttons that turns the camera, so you can steer and look at the same time. Dragging the arena still works and is unchanged. Arcade never shows it — that view's camera is fixed on purpose.
Drives the drag and the camera stick together, so one number is the answer to "the camera turns too fast".

Render

A skinned husk is real CPU, which is why they were cards. Auto is not "cards until proven otherwise" — it starts with ten models, which covers every enemy alive at once through wave 15, and only adds the rest once the measured 95th-percentile frame time proves there is room. It hands them back faster than it takes them.
Runs the same 26-husk load twice — once as meshes, once as cards — and reports percentiles, so the cost of the models is a number instead of an impression. Keep the window in front or the run invalidates itself.

Shroud

Motion

Nothing here changes the fight — only how much it moves. The hit vignette never turns off completely, because it is the only full-screen sign that something got through. Impact freeze is deliberately left alone: it is the absence of movement, and it is what tells you a hit landed once the shake is gone.

Presets & reset

A preset remembers how the game looks and feels — camera framing, fog, the pixel grid, the Shroud colours, the motion sliders. It deliberately does not remember the map, which view you are in, or how your pad is laid out, so loading one never moves you somewhere you did not ask to be.
Paste these to Claude and they become the new defaults.
